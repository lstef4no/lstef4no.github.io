NMPC-based driving copilot

High-performance virtual copilot

Building on an existing nonlinear model predictive controller for autonomous driving, this project explored its use as a virtual copilot.

My work involved adjustments to the existing NMPC, with the main focus on human-in-the-loop testing and the interaction between the controller and a human driver.

The project was carried out in collaboration with VI-Grade. Tests with human drivers were conducted using their high-performance driving simulator to evaluate the copilot in an interactive driving environment.

02

Robotics & autonomy

Bosch Future Mobility Challenge

Autonomous driving, at 1:10 scale

An international student competition bringing autonomous driving and connectivity to miniature smart cities. Each team worked with a 1:10 scale vehicle navigating a shared urban environment.

My contribution focused on the embedded system: integrating sensors, combining their measurements using our team’s own sensor fusion algorithm, and tuning the embedded PID controller.

We tested the vehicle in real-world conditions and participated in the competition finals at the Bosch centre in Cluj-Napoca, Romania.

Path planning for fixed-wing UAVs

From feasible trajectories to flight tests

A fixed-wing aircraft can’t simply stop or turn on the spot. Planning a path through obstacles means accounting for the vehicle’s motion constraints from the start.

For my thesis internship, I developed a Kinodynamic RRT* algorithm for online path planning, based on optimal control. A mathematical model of the aircraft’s kinematics and dynamics was used to account for its motion constraints and generate collision-free, dynamically feasible trajectories.

The approach was validated through real-world UAV flight tests at Calvert Vaux Park in New York City, bringing the mathematical model and planning algorithm together in a practical flight setting.
